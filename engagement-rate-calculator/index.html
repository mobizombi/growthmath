Engagement rate formulas

Engagement rate by followers = (Likes + Comments + Shares + Saves) / Posts / Followers x 100
Engagement rate by reach = (Likes + Comments + Shares + Saves) / Total reach x 100

By followers is the number anyone can calculate from public data, so it is what most brands and agencies quote. By reach measures how people who actually saw the post reacted - it is the fairer measure of content quality, because reach on most platforms is now driven by recommendations rather than by followers.

Worked example

Five posts collected 4,200 likes, 180 comments, 90 shares and 130 saves - 4,600 interactions, or 920 per post. With 50,000 followers that is 920 / 50,000 = 1.84% engagement by followers. The posts reached 60,000 people in total, so engagement by reach is 4,600 / 60,000 = 7.67%. Average reach per post was only 12,000, or 24% of followers - typical for an account that depends on followers rather than recommendations.

Ranges differ between studies, niches and account sizes - use them as a sanity check, not a target. Compare yourself mainly with your own past months and with accounts of a similar size in your niche.

Why small accounts have higher engagement

Engagement rate almost always falls as follower count rises. Small accounts have a tighter, more interested audience; large accounts collect inactive and passive followers over time. A 6% rate at 5,000 followers and a 1.5% rate at 500,000 followers can be equally healthy, which is why brands increasingly look at engagement within a follower band.

Which interactions matter most

  • Shares and saves signal that a post is worth coming back to or sending to someone - the platforms weight them heavily when deciding what to recommend.
  • Comments show conversation. A low comments-per-100-likes number on high likes can mean the content is passive, or that likes are not coming from real fans.
  • Likes are the cheapest action and the easiest to inflate.

Spotting fake engagement

Very high likes with almost no comments, generic one-word comments, or a sudden jump in followers without a matching jump in reach are classic signs of bought engagement. If you are vetting an influencer, ask for screenshots of reach and audience location from their insights, and estimate what a sponsored post is worth with the CPM calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good engagement rate on Instagram?

For most accounts 1% to 3% by followers is solid, and above 3% is strong. Smaller accounts usually see higher rates than large ones, and Reels tend to beat static posts.

Should I calculate engagement rate by followers or by reach?

Use followers when you only have public data or need to compare accounts. Use reach when you have insights access - it shows how well the content performs with the people who actually saw it.

Do views count as engagement?

Not in this formula. Views and impressions measure exposure, not interaction. Video creators sometimes report engagement by views instead of followers; in that case divide interactions by views.
